A place and a people

Where the water
flows fast.

The name Kishechiwan speaks to the swift current of the Albany River. The spelling “Kashechewan” came with the community’s first post-office sign and became its official name.1

Treaty 9 territory

The James Bay Treaty forms part of the history of the Omushkego Cree along the Albany River and James Bay.3

Understand Treaty 9
1958–1961

A community takes shape

Most Anglican families of Fort Albany moved across the river, forming the community that became Kashechewan.1

Our own Band Council

Kashechewan gained its own band council, while continuing to share the Fort Albany 67 reserve with Fort Albany First Nation.1

Language, land
& everyday life.

Cree is spoken at home, carried in stories, and shared through the knowledge of the land.

The seasonal goose hunt brings families onto the land and connects generations through skills, language, and kinship. These living traditions are part of the community’s everyday life.1
