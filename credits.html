Community identity

The Kashechewan logo

The community’s logo is reproduced unchanged from Mushkegowuk Council’s Nations page. The interconnected goose, moose, and human profile are also identified in Kashechewan’s community planning publication.

The logo belongs to its rights holders and is not covered by the open licences listed for the photographs below.

None of these photographs were taken by or for Kashechewan First Nation. They were sourced from NASA, Wikimedia Commons, and the Archives of Ontario because their licences allow reuse with attribution.

The photos below are of Fort Albany First Nation, not Kashechewan itself. Kashechewan and Fort Albany were one community until families split across the Albany River in 1958–1961, and the two First Nations still share a single reserve, Fort Albany 67, today — so Fort Albany's community and history are directly part of Kashechewan's own story. Captions say "Fort Albany," not Kashechewan, to stay accurate about what's actually pictured.

Kashechewan First Nation

An Omushkego (Swampy Cree) community on the Albany River, west coast of James Bay, Treaty 9, Northern Ontario.

Kashechewan First Nation is located on the traditional territory of the Omushkego Cree, within the lands of Treaty 9. We honour our ancestors, the land, and the waters of the Albany River and James Bay that sustain our people.
