Health & Wellness

Primary care and public-health services for the community are delivered through the local health centre. Because Kashechewan sits on a flood plain, health services are closely tied to emergency planning: infrastructure failures and flooding have, at times, forced the evacuation of patients and medical staff and the temporary closure of the community's only healthcare facility.1

In 2025, a water-system emergency and concerns over Cryptosporidium in the drinking water led to evacuations and testing before residents could safely return home.3 Clean, reliable drinking water remains one of the community's most urgent priorities.

Education

Kashechewan opened a new 24-classroom school in 2019. Funding was approved in December 2018 after the community declared a state of emergency in August 2018 over the health and safety of its aging portable school buildings.2

Built to move. The new school was constructed on pilings so that it can be relocated with the community to higher ground in the future — a design that reflects Kashechewan's long-term plan to move off the flood plain.2

Housing & Public Works

Housing and public infrastructure in Kashechewan are shaped by the constant risk of spring flooding. New housing units have been built in modular form so that they, too, can be moved to higher ground when the community relocates.2 Public works crews maintain roads, water and sewer systems, and the dike infrastructure that protects the community during break-up.

Emergency Management & Spring Flooding

Kashechewan lies on the flood plain of the Albany River, and many of its buildings are vulnerable to flooding when the river ice breaks up in spring. The community experienced two major evacuations in 2005, and spring evacuations have recurred many times since.1

Each spring, the community, Mushkegowuk Council, and partner agencies monitor conditions on the river and prepare for possible evacuation of residents to host communities in the south.

During an emergency: official evacuation notices and instructions come from Kashechewan First Nation and its emergency partners. This website should link to those official notices — add current emergency contact numbers and instructions here, and post updates on the News page. Do not rely on unofficial sources during a flood.

Relocation to Higher Ground

After decades of flooding, Kashechewan is working toward a long-term solution: relocating the community to higher ground, in an area roughly 30 kilometres to the north (referred to in planning as “Site 5”).2

May 2019

A Framework Agreement is signed by the federal Minister of Indigenous Services, Kashechewan's Chief, and Ontario's Minister of Indigenous Affairs, affirming the shared goal of relocating the community to higher ground.2

2019 onward

New community buildings — including the school — are designed on pilings and in modular form so they can move with the community.2

December 2025

Indigenous Services Canada approves approximately $8.4 million for detailed planning studies to advance the relocation.4

Relocation is a generational undertaking that touches every part of community life — housing, schools, health, water, and the connection between people and the land. Kashechewan's leadership continues to call for the federal and provincial governments to move the work forward without delay.4
